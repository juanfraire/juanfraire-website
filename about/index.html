About me

I'm a researcher at Inria, in the Agora team in Lyon, France. I am also with CONICET and the National University of Córdoba (UNC) in Argentina, where I did my PhD, and at Saarland University in Germany, where I belong to Holger Hermanns's Dependable Systems and Software group as a guest professor.

I study networks that reach beyond Earth. How do you route data when links come and go, and a signal to Mars takes minutes? How can a cheap sensor on a farm talk straight to a satellite? How do you run a constellation of thousands of spacecraft joined by lasers? And what does it cost, in mass, energy and carbon, to put computers in orbit? These questions are my four research topics.

It started with a real system. During my PhD I was the network architect of SARE, a satellite constellation of CONAE, the Argentine space agency, and I have stayed close to flight since. My routing code runs in NASA's ION and HDTN, our work was demonstrated on GomSpace's GomX-4 satellites and ESA's OPS-SAT, and I have carried out research stays at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory.

I chair the SPACE Research Group of the IRTF, where the Internet community works on networking beyond Earth. I co-founded the STINT workshop in 2014 and have chaired all 13 editions, and in France I run NTN Days, a conference on non-terrestrial networks. With students and colleagues on four continents, I have published more than 160 papers.
