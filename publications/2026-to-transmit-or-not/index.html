To Transmit or Not: Tiny On-Device Transformers for Uplink Prediction in Direct-to-Satellite IoT

Alexander Y. Choquenaira-Florez, Juan A. Fraire, and Hervé Rivano

IEEE Latin-American Conference on Communications (LATINCOM), 2026

ShareLinkedInXBlueskyEmail

Abstract

Direct-to-Satellite IoT (DtS-IoT) promises to connect massive fleets of devices in regions where terrestrial infrastructure will never reach. Its weakest link, however, is the device itself: DtS-IoT nodes are inexpensive, energy-constrained, and effectively blind, since downlink feedback from sporadically passing Low-Earth Orbit (LEO) satellites is broadcast, slow, and costly, so in practice devices cannot expect their transmissions to be acknowledged. The satellite cannot micromanage its fleet: every transmission decision must be made on-device, based solely on local information, and every wasted uplink drains energy and fuels collisions. We argue that these decisions can be learned, provided the models are small enough to run on highly constrained hardware and to be updated over a scarce downlink. This paper presents a tiny encoder-only Transformer (14k parameters, 64 KB) that predicts, from the device's recent transmission context, whether an uplink will be received. To address the scarcity of DtS-IoT data, we generate synthetic datasets using the open-source FLoRaSat simulator and introduce an ML Sandbox module that embeds trained models directly into the simulation for closed-loop evaluation. Deployed in that closed loop, the model delivers 16.1% more unique packets than an unguided baseline while sending 41.6% fewer transmissions, and its operating frontier dominates a Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (Bi-LSTM) of comparable footprint, an analytical link-budget policy, and the blind repetition budget itself: spending the same energy without the model delivers 2.3× fewer packets. Both gains are the currency that matters for blind, battery-powered devices: more data through, less energy spent.

  1. DORSAL: Downlink Optimization for Robust Direct-to-Satellite LoRaWAN Juan A. Fraire, Oana Iova, Carlos Fernandez Hernandez, Fabrice Valois, Hervé Rivano, Cesar Azurdia-Meza, Marcos Diaz, Miguel Gutiérrez Gaitán, Diego Dujovne, Samuel Montejo Sánchez, and Richard Demo Souza IEEE Internet of Things Journal, 2026
    Abstract

    Delivering downlink (DL) ACKs to Class A LoRaWAN devices via low Earth orbit (LEO) satellite constellations requires predicting end-to-end propagation delays of 10–500 ms to hit a 1-s receive window, a timing challenge absent from terrestrial schedulers. We present downlink optimization for robust direct-to-satellite LoRaWAN (DORSAL), the first mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) scheduler for DL delivery in direct-to-satellite (DtS) LoRaWAN networks. It introduces the downlink arrival receive-window timing (DART) as the central constraint to jointly optimize gateway (GW) selection and packet injection timing across ISL-capable and bent-pipe architectures. Evaluation on a Walker 5× 5 polar constellation (25 satellites, 650 km) and a commercial ground station (GS) network yields five results: 1) DART infeasibility blocks 73% of bent-pipe deliveries regardless of scheduler quality: DART-unaware approaches collapse from 93% nominal to only 20% real delivery; 2) ISL routing raises %ACK by + 67.4 pp over bent-pipe (91.6% versus 24.2% at n_mathrm GS=3 ground stations, ISM band) by eliminating the three-way simultaneous-visibility constraint, with 73% of ACKs delivered via ≥ 2 -hop paths; 3) ISL %ACK is nearly flat across n_mathrm GSin 3,6,12 GSs (91.6%–92.7%), showing that a minimal three-station polar network captures the full scheduling benefit of an ISL-equipped constellation; 4) ISL hardware speed has negligible impact up to 300 ms/hop: %ACK is flat from 0 to 300 ms, then degrades in two discrete steps (−5.3 pp at 500 ms, −2 pp at 700 ms) as successive hop-count tiers lose causal injection feasibility; and 5) the MILP solves to proven optimality within seconds for up to 10^3 devices, confirming that the sparse conflict-graph structure keeps the problem tractable at realistic Internet of Things (IoT) subscriber counts.

  2. On the role of machine learning in satellite internet of things: A survey of techniques, challenges, and future directions Alexander Ylnner Choquenaira Florez, Juan A. Fraire, Hannah B. Pasandi, and Hervé Rivano Elsevier Computer Networks, 2025
    Abstract

    The drive towards an interconnected world via satellites is reshaping the landscape of communication technologies. This survey comprehensively reviews studies in the Satellite Internet of Things (SIoT) domain, focusing on the role of Machine Learning (ML) techniques. Indeed, the global data collection scale in SIoT is ideally suited for data-intensive and sophisticated ML approaches. We highlight the innovative use of ML to address specific SIoT challenges, aiming to identify current trends, methodologies, and outcomes. We considered theoretical, practical, and experimental research, organizing existing publications into a new taxonomy that intersects ML and SIoT categories. Our taxonomy reveals that Deep Learning (DL), Reinforcement Learning (RL), and Federated Learning (FL) are widely applied to address radio access schemes, resource and network management, and application-specific issues. This survey identifies critical gaps in current research on ML applications in SIoT, such as the lack of differentiation between space-based and ground-based processing, insufficient integration of SIoT-specific metrics, and the oversight of limited computational resources on orbiting satellites. These issues raise concerns about the feasibility and efficiency of proposed solutions. We propose promising research directions based on the derived insights to effectively bridge the gap between ML researchers and industrial SIoT entities.

  3. Space-Terrestrial Integrated Internet of Things: Challenges and Opportunities Juan A. Fraire, Oana Iova, and Fabrice Valois IEEE Communications Magazine, 2022
    Abstract

    Large geographical regions of our planet remain uncovered by terrestrial network connections. Sparse and dense constellations of near Earth orbit satellites can bridge this gap by providing Internet of Things (IoT) connectivity on a worldwide scale in a flexible and cost-effective manner. This article presents STEREO: a novel space-terrestrial integrated IoT architecture spanning direct- and indirect-to-satellite access from IoT assets on the surface. Framed on the identified requirements, we analyze NB-IoT and LoRa/LoRaWAN features to put these technologies forward as appealing candidates for future satellite IoT deployments. Finally, we list and discuss the key open research challenges to be addressed in order to achieve successful space-terrestrial IoT integration.
